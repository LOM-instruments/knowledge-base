<h2>User manual</h2>
</div>

<div class="prose-block" markdown>

!!! warning "Read this before first use"

    - **Never open or disassemble the sensors.** There are no user-serviceable parts inside, and opening them voids the warranty.
    - **Protect the sensors from water and moisture.** They are not waterproof. If they get damp, let them dry fully at room temperature before using them again.
    - **Handle the plastic case with care.** Use only the [Uši mount with the lyre](https://store.lom.audio/products/usi-microphone-mount-single) for mounting — other mounts may damage the case.
    - **Mind the temperature.** Operate only between −10 °C and +55 °C, and don't leave the sensors in direct sun or a parked car.
    - **Protect your hearing.** Electromagnetic signals can jump in level without warning — when monitoring on headphones, start at low volume and raise it gradually.

**Will it work with my setup?**

Elektrouši are fully passive — they generate a faint signal that needs a microphone preamplifier. Two ways to use them: plug them into the input of an **Elektrosluch 2, 3, or 3+** for handheld listening with an extended sensing range, or connect them straight to the **3.5 mm stereo microphone input** of a portable recorder. Plug-in power inputs are fine — the sensors don't need the power, but it does them no harm.

**Quick start**

1. Plug Elektrouši into the Elektrosluch input, or into your recorder's 3.5 mm stereo mic input.
2. Set your gain: start low and raise it until the fields you're exploring sit at a healthy level.
3. Move the sensors close to electronics, power supplies, motors, displays, or transit infrastructure — the electromagnetic fields around them are the signal. Being a stereo pair, the two sensors can frame a source from different sides for a spatial image.

**Mounting**

Elektrouši can be mounted with the [Uši mount](https://store.lom.audio/products/usi-microphone-mount-single) — but only the lyre version, and even then take care not to stress the plastic case. See [the FAQ](../faq/electromagnetic.md#elektrousi-mount).

<h2>Applications</h2>
</div>

<div class="prose-block" markdown>

Elektrouši listen to the inaudible electromagnetic side of the world: electronics, power lines, motors, displays, and transit systems. Compared to [Priezor](priezor.md)'s ambient response, Elektrouši are more directional — better suited to pinpointing and framing individual sources — and, like Elektrosluch, they work in stereo. Paired with an Elektrosluch they extend its reach into spots the device itself can't go; paired with a recorder they turn any plug-in-power input into an electromagnetic stereo rig. See [the differences between the electromagnetic instruments](../faq/electromagnetic.md#difference-em).

</div>

<div class="banner">
<h2>Care & maintenance</h2>
</div>

<div class="prose-block" markdown>

**Storage.** Keep the sensors in a closed case or pouch, away from dust and humidity. Coil the cable loosely — sharp bends shorten its life.

**Cleaning.** Wipe the plastic bodies with a dry or slightly damp cloth. Never use solvents.

**Moisture.** The sensors are not waterproof. If they get damp, let them dry fully at room temperature before the next session.

</div>

<div class="banner dark">
<h2>Troubleshooting</h2>
</div>

<div class="prose-block" markdown>

- **No sound, or a very quiet signal** → Elektrouši are passive and output a faint, microphone-level signal: make sure you're in a *mic* (not line) input and give it generous gain.
- **Only one channel records** → check the minijack is fully seated and the recorder isn't set to mono.
- **Hum and buzz everywhere** → that's not a fault — mains hum and switching noise *are* the electromagnetic landscape the sensors are built to reveal. Move the sensors, and the character changes.
- Something else? [See all electromagnetic FAQs →](../faq/electromagnetic.md)

</div>
